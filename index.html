Module 1

Cybersecurity Fundamentals

🛡

What is Cybersecurity?

Protecting devices, accounts, networks, and data from digital attacks.

In simple terms: Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting your computers, phones, online accounts, and personal information from hackers, scammers, and malicious software. It covers everything from the password on your phone to the firewall on a company server.
Example: Using a strong password and two-factor authentication on your email stops attackers from reading your messages or resetting your other accounts.
Remember: Cybersecurity isn't just for tech experts — everyone who uses the internet needs it.
⚡

Types of Cyber Threats

Malware, phishing, ransomware, social engineering, and more.

Main threat categories: Phishing (fake messages), Malware (harmful software), Ransomware (locks your files for money), Social Engineering (psychological tricks), Man-in-the-Middle (intercepting communications), DDoS (overloading a system), and Zero-Day Exploits (attacking unknown vulnerabilities).
Example: A fake email pretending to be from your bank is phishing. A program that secretly records your keystrokes is spyware (a type of malware).
🎯

Why People Get Hacked

Weak passwords, clicking bad links, reusing credentials, trusting strangers.

The main reasons: Reusing passwords across sites, clicking links without checking them, downloading files from untrusted sources, sharing personal information on social media, ignoring software updates, and falling for urgency-based scam messages.
Golden rule: Pause before you click. Scammers rely on speed — they want you to act before you think.
🔺

The CIA Triad

Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability — the foundation of security.

Confidentiality: Only authorized people can access data. Integrity: Data hasn't been tampered with. Availability: Systems and data are accessible when needed. Every security decision maps back to protecting one or more of these three pillars.
Example: Encrypting your messages protects confidentiality. A checksum on a download protects integrity. A backup protects availability.
Module 2

Scams & Social Engineering

🎭

What is Social Engineering?

Manipulating people using psychology instead of hacking technology.

Social engineering attacks exploit human trust, fear, curiosity, and urgency. Attackers impersonate trusted figures (banks, tech support, friends) to trick you into sharing information, clicking links, or sending money. No software vulnerability needed — you are the target.
Example: "Your account will be closed in 24 hours unless you verify your identity now." This uses fear and urgency together.
🎣

Phishing Attacks

Fake emails and messages that steal your login credentials.

Phishing messages pretend to be from real companies (Netflix, Amazon, your bank). They contain links to fake websites that look identical to real ones. When you enter your password, the attacker captures it. Always check the sender email and the URL carefully.
How to spot it: Hover over links before clicking. Check for misspelled domains (amaz0n.com, paypaI.com). Real companies never ask for your password by email.
📱

Vishing & Smishing

Phone call scams and SMS text message scams.

Vishing (voice phishing) uses phone calls — attackers pose as bank reps, government officials, or tech support. Smishing uses text messages with malicious links. Both create panic: "Your account has been compromised" or "You owe money to the government."
Defense: Never give personal information over a call you didn't initiate. Hang up and call the official number directly.
💔

Romance & Dating Scams

Fake relationships built online to steal money.

Scammers create attractive fake profiles on dating sites and social media. They build emotional connections over weeks or months, then ask for money citing emergencies — medical bills, travel costs, business problems. They refuse video calls and always have excuses.
Red flags: They profess love very quickly, can never meet in person, always have financial emergencies, and resist video calls.
🖥

Tech Support Scams

Fake virus warnings that trick you into paying for "fixes."

Popup messages claim your computer is infected and tell you to call a number. The "technician" asks for remote access and then installs malware, steals data, or charges hundreds of dollars for fake repairs. Real companies like Microsoft never show popups asking you to call them.
What to do: Close the browser tab. Never call the number. Run your own antivirus scan.
💰

Investment & Crypto Scams

Fake investment platforms that promise guaranteed high returns.

Scammers create professional-looking websites offering crypto trading, forex, or investment opportunities with "guaranteed" 300%+ returns. They show fake dashboards with growing balances. When you try to withdraw, they ask for more "fees" — and the money is gone.
Rule: If it sounds too good to be true, it is. No legitimate investment guarantees returns. Check if the platform is registered with financial regulators.
🔐

What You Should Never Share

OTPs, passwords, PINs, banking details, and ID numbers.

Never share: OTP codes, passwords, PINs, bank card numbers, CVV codes, ID/passport numbers, recovery codes, or private keys. No legitimate company will ever ask for these via email, phone, or text. If someone asks, it's a scam — no exceptions.
Module 3

Malware Deep Dive

🦠

What is Malware?

Malicious software designed to damage, spy, or steal.

Malware is any software intentionally designed to cause harm. It can steal your passwords, encrypt your files for ransom, spy on your activity, display unwanted ads, or give attackers remote control of your device. It spreads through downloads, email attachments, infected websites, and USB drives.
🐛

Viruses & Worms

Self-replicating programs that spread across devices.

Viruses attach to legitimate files and activate when you open them. They need human action to spread. Worms spread automatically across networks without any user interaction. Both can corrupt files, slow down systems, and open backdoors for other attacks.
Example: The ILOVEYOU worm (2000) spread via email and caused $10 billion in damage worldwide.
🐴

Trojans & RATs

Malware disguised as legitimate software that opens backdoors.

Trojans pretend to be useful programs (free games, cracked software, fake updates) but secretly install malware. RATs (Remote Access Trojans) give attackers full control of your device — they can watch your screen, access your camera, steal files, and log keystrokes.
Prevention: Only download software from official sources. If a paid app is offered free somewhere, it's probably a trojan.
🔒

Ransomware

Encrypts your files and demands payment to unlock them.

Ransomware encrypts all your files (documents, photos, databases) and displays a message demanding payment (usually in Bitcoin). Even paying doesn't guarantee you'll get your files back. It spreads through phishing emails, infected downloads, and unpatched software vulnerabilities.
Best defense: Regular backups stored offline. If hit, disconnect immediately, don't pay, report to authorities, and restore from backup.
👁

Spyware & Keyloggers

Silently monitors everything you do and type.

Spyware monitors your browsing, captures screenshots, and tracks your location. Keyloggers record every keystroke — passwords, messages, credit card numbers. They often come bundled with free software or are installed through phishing attacks.
Signs of infection: Device running slower than usual, battery draining fast, unfamiliar apps installed, increased data usage.
🧹

How to Remove Malware

Step-by-step: disconnect, scan, remove, recover.

Step 1: Disconnect from the internet immediately. Step 2: Boot into Safe Mode. Step 3: Run a full antivirus scan. Step 4: Delete suspicious programs and files. Step 5: Change all passwords from a clean device. Step 6: Update all software. Step 7: Monitor accounts for unusual activity.
Module 4

Passwords & Authentication

🔑

Creating Strong Passwords

Long, random, unique for every account.

A strong password is at least 12 characters with a mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Never use personal info (names, birthdays, pet names). Use passphrases: random words combined like "Falcon!River92#Sky" are strong and memorable.
Test: If someone who knows you could guess it, it's not strong enough.
🗄

Password Managers

Securely store and generate unique passwords for every account.

Password managers (Bitwarden, 1Password, KeePass) store all your passwords in an encrypted vault. You only need to remember one master password. They auto-generate strong unique passwords for each site, auto-fill login forms, and alert you about breached passwords.
📲

Two-Factor Authentication

A second verification step that blocks unauthorized access.

2FA adds a second layer after your password — a code from an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy), a hardware key, or an SMS code. Even if your password is stolen, the attacker can't get in without the second factor.
Best practice: Use an authenticator app over SMS. SIM-swap attacks can intercept text messages.
⚔

Common Password Attacks

How attackers crack and steal passwords.

Brute force: Trying every possible combination. Dictionary: Trying common words and phrases. Credential stuffing: Using leaked passwords from other sites. Phishing: Tricking you into typing your password on a fake site. Keylogging: Recording your keystrokes.
Defense: Unique passwords per site + 2FA defeats almost all of these attacks.
Module 5

Safe Browsing & Networks

🔗

HTTPS & Encryption

The padlock icon and what it actually means.

HTTPS encrypts data between your browser and the website. The "S" stands for Secure. Look for the padlock icon in the address bar. However, HTTPS alone doesn't mean the site is legitimate — scam sites can also use HTTPS. Always verify the domain name too.
📡

Public Wi-Fi Dangers

Why coffee shop Wi-Fi can be a trap.

On public Wi-Fi, attackers can intercept unencrypted traffic, capture login credentials, inject malware into downloads, or redirect you to fake websites. Never do banking, enter passwords, or access sensitive accounts on public networks.
Stay safe: Use mobile data for sensitive tasks. If you must use public Wi-Fi, connect through a trusted VPN.
🌐

VPNs Explained

An encrypted tunnel that protects your internet traffic.

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) encrypts all your internet traffic and routes it through a secure server. This hides your activity from your ISP, protects you on public Wi-Fi, and can help bypass geographic restrictions. Choose reputable paid VPNs — free VPNs often sell your data.
👿

Evil Twin Attacks

Fake Wi-Fi networks that look like the real thing.

Attackers create Wi-Fi networks with names identical to legitimate ones: "Airport_Free_WiFi" or "Starbucks_Guest." When you connect, all your traffic passes through the attacker's device. They can see everything you do — passwords, emails, banking details.
Defense: Ask staff for the exact network name. Turn off auto-join for Wi-Fi networks. Use a VPN.
Module 6

Privacy & Data Protection

📋

What is Personal Data?

Name, phone, email, location, photos, passwords, and more.

Personal data includes anything that can identify you: full name, phone number, email, home address, location data, photos, date of birth, ID numbers, financial information, biometric data, and even your browsing history. Every piece makes you more vulnerable to targeted attacks.
⚙

App Permissions

Why a calculator shouldn't need your microphone.

Every app permission is a door to your data. Review what each app can access: camera, microphone, contacts, location, storage, phone. Only grant what the app genuinely needs. A flashlight app needing your contacts is a red flag. Regularly audit permissions in your phone settings.
💧

Data Breaches

When companies lose your data to hackers.

Data breaches happen when hackers steal databases from companies — exposing millions of emails, passwords, and personal details. Check if your email has been compromised at haveibeenpwned.com. If breached, change that password everywhere you used it (another reason to never reuse passwords).
👣

Your Digital Footprint

Everything you do online leaves a trace.

Every post, search, like, purchase, and login contributes to your digital footprint. Employers check it. Scammers mine it. Advertisers profile it. Be intentional: review privacy settings, think before posting, use private browsing for sensitive searches, and periodically Google yourself.
Module 7

Prevention & Response

✅

Daily Security Checklist

Simple habits that prevent most attacks.

Daily: Don't click suspicious links. Lock your devices. Weekly: Review app permissions. Check for software updates. Monthly: Review account activity. Check for data breaches. Change critical passwords. Always: Use unique passwords + 2FA. Back up important files. Think before you share.
🚨

What To Do If Hacked

Immediate steps to contain the damage.

1. Change passwords immediately (start with email). 2. Enable 2FA on everything. 3. Log out of all devices. 4. Check for unauthorized transactions. 5. Scan devices for malware. 6. Notify your bank if financial data was exposed. 7. Alert contacts if your social accounts were compromised. 8. Report to the platform and authorities.
📢

Reporting Cyber Crime

Where and how to report attacks and fraud.

Report phishing emails to the company being impersonated. Report scams to your local police cybercrime unit. In the US, report to IC3.gov (FBI). In the UAE, contact the Cybercrime Unit. Save evidence: screenshots, emails, transaction records. The sooner you report, the better the chance of recovery.
🔄

Staying Updated & Safe

Software updates close the doors that hackers use.

Software updates patch security vulnerabilities that attackers exploit. Enable automatic updates on your phone, computer, and apps. Use reputable antivirus software. Stay informed about new scam techniques. Follow cybersecurity news. The threat landscape evolves — your defenses should too.
