Signs a text is a scam

  • Urgency or a threat: "pay today", "your account will be closed", "final notice".
  • A link to pay a small fee for a parcel, a toll or a fine.
  • A web address that is almost right: extra words, swapped letters or an odd ending, such as dhl-redelivery-fee.example (made up).
  • A request for a code sent to your phone, a password or card details.
  • A prize, refund or job you never applied for.
  • A new number claiming to be a family member who "lost their phone".

One sign is enough to stop and check. Scammers change the wording all the time, so a message can be a scam even if it looks clean.

What CyberNet AI checks

Quick Scan reads the words and any links in the message: pressure and payment requests, look-alike web addresses, link shorteners, risky web address endings and common scam scripts in English and Arabic. You get a verdict and the reasons, so you can judge it yourself.

It cannot see where a message really came from, and no checker can promise a message is safe. When in doubt, contact the company or person another way.

Already clicked, paid or shared a code?

  • Paid or typed card details: call the number on the back of your card now. Ask your bank to block the card and dispute the payment.
  • Typed a password: change it, and change it anywhere else you used it. Turn on two-step sign-in.
  • Shared a code sent to your phone: tell that company or bank straight away, using its official app or number.
  • Installed an app or gave remote access: remove it, and get help before you use online banking on that device.

Where to report it

  • UAE: report it to the police through the Dubai Police eCrime platform (ecrimehub.gov.ae), the Dubai Police app, or your emirate's police app.
  • United States: forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) and report them at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • United Kingdom: forward scam texts to 7726 and emails to report@phishing.gov.uk; report losses to Report Fraud.
  • Elsewhere: contact your bank and the police. Then block the sender.
