Black Friday scam checklist

Ten quick checks before you buy, pay or tap a link this Black Friday, Cyber Monday and holiday delivery season. If something fails a check, stop and look again.

  1. Read the store's address letter by letter

    Fake shops copy real ones with addresses like amaz0n-deals.shop or walmart-blackfriday.store. Watch for swapped letters, extra words and odd endings. A shop that only appeared a few weeks ago is a warning sign. Type the store's address yourself or use its official app.

  2. Be wary of a deal far below every other shop

    Black Friday prices are low, but not impossible. A $1,200 phone for $199 or a $700 TV for $89 is bait. Compare the price at two or three well-known stores. If it is far cheaper than all of them, walk away.

  3. Ignore parcel texts asking for a "small fee"

    "Your package is on hold. Pay a $1.99 redelivery fee." These texts are a favorite in December. Carriers like USPS, UPS and FedEx do not text you a link to pay a surprise fee. In the UAE the same trick uses the names of Emirates Post and Aramex. How to spot a fake parcel text. Track your order with the tracking number in the store's order email, on the carrier's own site or app.

  4. Look twice at QR codes on meters and posters

    Scammers stick their own QR codes over real ones on parking meters, EV chargers and posters. If a code is a sticker, or the page asks for card details straight away, do not pay through it. Use the official parking app or the address printed on the machine.

  5. Nobody real asks to be paid in gift cards

    Stores, delivery companies, the IRS, banks and the police never ask you to pay with gift cards. Anyone who wants you to buy cards and read out the numbers is a scammer, every time.

  6. Check refund and support pages and phone numbers

    Fake "customer support" pages and numbers show up in search results and ads. Get the number from the store's official app or website, or from your order email. A real agent will never ask you to install remote-access software or read out a code sent to your phone.

  7. Toll and fine texts are usually fake

    "Unpaid toll of $4.35. Pay today to avoid a $50 fine." Texts like this pretend to come from E-ZPass, SunPass or the DMV, and in the UAE from Salik, RTA or DARB. Salik says its real messages come only from the sender name SALIK. How to check a toll or fine text. Do not tap the link. Check by typing your toll agency's official website yourself, or calling the number on your account.

  8. Walk away from sellers who want bank transfer or crypto only

    A seller who only takes a bank transfer, Zelle, a wire or cryptocurrency is asking for money you cannot get back. Pay with a credit card or a checkout that offers buyer protection.

  9. Check reviews and company details

    Look for reviews on other sites, not just the shop's own page. A real store lists a street address, a phone number and a clear returns policy. Search the store's name with the word "scam" before you pay.

  10. Already paid? Act now

    Call the number on the back of your card and ask your bank to stop or dispute the payment. If you typed a password on the site, change it, and change it anywhere else you used it.
