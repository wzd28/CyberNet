Find the real website in any link

Look at the part between https:// and the first single /. The real website is the last two parts of that, read from the right.

  • paypal.com.account-verify.example/login is the website account-verify.example (a made-up address). "paypal.com" at the front is decoration.
  • amazon.secure-help.example is secure-help.example, not Amazon.
  • www.amazon.com/orders is amazon.com.

Some countries add a part, such as .co.uk or .gov.ae, so there the website is the last three parts.

Warning signs in a link

  1. A look-alike name

    Swapped or extra letters, numbers for letters (amaz0n), extra words such as "secure", "verify" or "support", or letters from another alphabet that look the same.

  2. A link shortener

    bit.ly and similar hide the real address. Check where it really goes before you open it.

  3. An unusual ending

    Endings such as .top, .xyz or .shop are not bad in themselves, but a "bank" or "courier" on one is a warning sign.

  4. It came with pressure

    A link that arrives with a threat, a prize or a fee deserves a check, whatever it looks like.

The padlock in the address bar only means the connection is encrypted. Scam sites have padlocks too.

Already clicked, paid or shared a code?

  • Paid or typed card details: call the number on the back of your card now. Ask your bank to block the card and dispute the payment.
  • Typed a password: change it, and change it anywhere else you used it. Turn on two-step sign-in.
  • Shared a code sent to your phone: tell that company or bank straight away, using its official app or number.
  • Installed an app or gave remote access: remove it, and get help before you use online banking on that device.

Where to report it

  • UAE: report it to the police through the Dubai Police eCrime platform (ecrimehub.gov.ae), the Dubai Police app, or your emirate's police app.
  • United States: forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) and report them at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • United Kingdom: forward scam texts to 7726 and emails to report@phishing.gov.uk; report losses to Report Fraud.
  • Elsewhere: contact your bank and the police. Then block the sender.

Questions people ask

How can I check if a link is safe without clicking it?

Long-press the link, choose Copy Link and paste it into CyberNet AI's free Quick Scan. It checks the address for warning signs without opening the page.

Does the padlock mean a website is safe?

No. The padlock only means the connection is encrypted. Scam sites use padlocks too.

What is a look-alike link?

A web address made to look like a real company's, with a swapped letter, an extra word or letters from another alphabet, such as amaz0n-support.example.

I opened a scam link. Am I hacked?

Opening a page is usually not enough on its own. The risk comes from typing a password, card details or a code, or installing something. If you did, change that password and call your bank.
