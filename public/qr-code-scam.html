QR code scams: check before you scan and pay

Scammers stick their own QR codes over real ones on parking meters, EV chargers, posters and menus, and print them in fake letters and emails. A QR code is just a hidden web address, so check where it goes before you pay.

Where fake QR codes turn up

  • Parking meters and EV chargers: a sticker over the real code that opens a fake payment page.
  • Restaurant tables and posters: a code that opens a page asking for card details or a login.
  • Emails and letters: "Scan to confirm your account" or "scan to update your delivery". A QR code in an email can get past email filters that check links.
  • Marketplace sales: a "buyer" sends a QR code so you can "receive" money. Scanning a code never pays you.

How to check a QR code

  1. Look at the code itself

    Is it a sticker on top of something? Is it peeling, crooked or covering another code? Do not use it.

  2. Read the address before it opens

    Your phone camera shows the web address first. Check the part just before the first single /. A short link or a strange name is a warning sign.

  3. Check it with CyberNet AI

    Take a photo or screenshot of the code and upload it to the free Quick Scan. It reads the hidden web address in the code and checks it for warning signs, without opening the page.

  4. Pay another way

    Use the official parking or payment app, or type the address printed on the machine.

Already clicked, paid or shared a code?

  • Paid or typed card details: call the number on the back of your card now. Ask your bank to block the card and dispute the payment.
  • Typed a password: change it, and change it anywhere else you used it. Turn on two-step sign-in.
  • Shared a code sent to your phone: tell that company or bank straight away, using its official app or number.
  • Installed an app or gave remote access: remove it, and get help before you use online banking on that device.

Where to report it

  • UAE: report it to the police through the Dubai Police eCrime platform (ecrimehub.gov.ae), the Dubai Police app, or your emirate's police app.
  • United States: forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) and report them at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • United Kingdom: forward scam texts to 7726 and emails to report@phishing.gov.uk; report losses to Report Fraud.
  • Elsewhere: contact your bank and the police. Then block the sender.

Questions people ask

Can scanning a QR code hack my phone?

Scanning only reads a web address. The danger is what you do next: typing card details or a password into a fake page, or installing an app from it.

How do I check a QR code without opening it?

Upload a photo or screenshot of the code to CyberNet AI's free Quick Scan. It reads the hidden address and shows the warning signs without opening the page.

Is a QR code on a parking meter safe to use?

Check that it is not a sticker on top of the machine, and read the address before you pay. The safer choice is the official parking app or the address printed on the meter.

Someone sent me a QR code to receive money. Is that a scam?

Yes. Scanning a QR code never pays you money. It is a common trick on marketplace sites to take a payment or your card details.
