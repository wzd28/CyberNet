Parcel delivery scam texts: how to spot a fake fee

"Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay a small fee to reschedule." This is one of the most common scam texts, and it spikes around shopping seasons. Here is how to tell a fake one.

What it looks like

Example scam message (made up, the address is fake)Emirates Post: Your shipment is on hold due to an incomplete address. Pay AED 4.50 to reschedule delivery: emiratespost-ae.redelivery-help.example

How the scam works

The text says a parcel is waiting and asks for a tiny payment: a redelivery fee, customs duty or an address update. The link opens a copy of a real courier's site. The fee is small so you pay without thinking, but the page takes your full card details and often the one-time code from your bank, which lets the scammer spend much more.

These texts pretend to come from Emirates Post, Aramex, DHL, FedEx, UPS, USPS, Royal Mail, Evri and Cyprus Post, among others. In January 2026 the UAE Cyber Security Council, Emirates Post and Abu Dhabi's DARB issued a joint warning about fake payment messages like these.

Signs the delivery text is fake

  1. You were not expecting a parcel

    Or you were, but the text names no shop, no order and no real tracking number.

  2. It asks you to pay through a link

    A surprise fee with a payment link is the core of this scam. The US Postal Inspection Service says USPS only texts or emails you after you ask for updates with a tracking number, and those messages do not contain a link.

  3. The web address is not the courier's own

    Look at the part just before the first single /. In emiratespost-ae.redelivery-help.example the real site is redelivery-help.example, not Emirates Post.

  4. It comes from a personal number or an email address

    Many fake texts arrive from ordinary phone numbers or iMessage email addresses, not the company's name.

  5. It pushes you to act within hours

    "Today only" or "your parcel will be returned" is there to stop you checking.

How to check safely

  • Do not tap the link. Paste the whole text into the free checker to see the warning signs.
  • Track the parcel yourself: use the tracking number in the shop's order email, on the courier's official app or a website address you type in.
  • If a real fee is due, the courier's own app or website will show it after you sign in.

Already clicked, paid or shared a code?

  • Paid or typed card details: call the number on the back of your card now. Ask your bank to block the card and dispute the payment.
  • Typed a password: change it, and change it anywhere else you used it. Turn on two-step sign-in.
  • Shared a code sent to your phone: tell that company or bank straight away, using its official app or number.
  • Installed an app or gave remote access: remove it, and get help before you use online banking on that device.

Where to report it

  • UAE: report it to the police through the Dubai Police eCrime platform (ecrimehub.gov.ae), the Dubai Police app, or your emirate's police app.
  • United States: forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) and report them at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • United Kingdom: forward scam texts to 7726 and emails to report@phishing.gov.uk; report losses to Report Fraud.
  • Elsewhere: contact your bank and the police. Then block the sender.

Questions people ask

Do couriers text you to pay a redelivery fee?

Treat any text asking for a surprise fee through a link as a scam until you have checked it in the courier's official app or a website address you typed yourself.

I paid the small fee. What now?

Call your bank on the number on the back of your card, say you paid on a scam site, and ask them to block the card. Do not wait for a bigger charge to appear.

Why is the fee so small?

A small amount makes you pay without thinking. The real goal is your full card details and the one-time code from your bank.

How do I check if a delivery text is real?

Paste it into CyberNet AI's free checker to see the warning signs, then track the parcel in the courier's official app with the tracking number from your order email.
