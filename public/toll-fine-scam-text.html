Fake toll and traffic fine texts: how to check

"You have an unpaid toll. Pay today to avoid a fine." Fake toll and fine texts are among the most common scam messages in the UAE, the US and Europe. Here is how to tell them apart from the real thing.

What it looks like

Example scam message (made up, the address is fake)SALIK: Your vehicle has an outstanding toll balance of AED 32. Pay within 24 hours to avoid a AED 400 penalty: salik-ae.pay-toll.example

Where these texts are going round

  • UAE: fake Salik, RTA, DARB and police fine messages. Salik says its real messages come only from the sender name SALIK. Dubai's RTA has warned about fake unpaid-fine messages, and the UAE Cyber Security Council has reported a rise in fraudulent messages.
  • United States: fake E-ZPass, SunPass, FasTrak and other toll texts. The FBI has warned about unpaid-toll texts and asks people to report them at ic3.gov.
  • Cyprus and Europe: Cyprus police warned in May 2026 about fake traffic-fine texts.

A sender name alone does not prove a message is real, because scammers can sometimes fake it. The link is what matters.

Signs the toll or fine text is fake

  1. A link to pay straight away

    The text sends you to a payment page and threatens a much bigger fine or a court case if you wait.

  2. The address is not the official site

    Look at the part just before the first single /. In salik-ae.pay-toll.example the real site is pay-toll.example, which has nothing to do with Salik.

  3. It names no plate number, date or place

    Or the details are vague, such as "your vehicle".

  4. You do not have an account with that toll company

    A toll text for a state or a country you have not driven in is fake.

How to check safely

  • Do not tap the link. Paste the text into the free checker to see the warning signs.
  • Check your balance or fines yourself in the official app, or on a website address you type in, such as your toll company's or police's own site.
  • If a real fine exists, it will show there. You never need the link in the text.

Already clicked, paid or shared a code?

  • Paid or typed card details: call the number on the back of your card now. Ask your bank to block the card and dispute the payment.
  • Typed a password: change it, and change it anywhere else you used it. Turn on two-step sign-in.
  • Shared a code sent to your phone: tell that company or bank straight away, using its official app or number.
  • Installed an app or gave remote access: remove it, and get help before you use online banking on that device.

Where to report it

  • UAE: report it to the police through the Dubai Police eCrime platform (ecrimehub.gov.ae), the Dubai Police app, or your emirate's police app.
  • United States: forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) and report them at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • United Kingdom: forward scam texts to 7726 and emails to report@phishing.gov.uk; report losses to Report Fraud.
  • Elsewhere: contact your bank and the police. Then block the sender.

Questions people ask

How do I know if a Salik text is real?

Salik says its real messages come only from the sender name SALIK. Even then, do not pay through a link in a text. Check your balance in the official Salik app or website.

Are unpaid toll texts in the US a scam?

Most are. The FBI has warned about fake unpaid-toll texts. Check your account on your toll agency's official website or app, typed in yourself.

I paid a fake toll or fine. What should I do?

Call your bank on the number on the back of your card and ask them to block the card and dispute the payment. Then report the text to the police.
