# Marketing video case studies, 2023-2026 (AI-made and viral)

> **How this was researched.** Web searches run on 2026-11-05; direct page fetching was blocked, so facts come from pages surfaced by search. Numbers are labelled by who claims them. "Self-reported" means the brand, agency or an award entry, not independent measurement. Where sources conflict, both figures are given.

Quick index: AI-made ads (1-8), CGI "FOOH" (9-10), low-cost native social (11-14). Failures/backlashes: 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 (and 7 partially).

---

## 1. Coca-Cola "Holidays Are Coming" AI remakes (2024 and 2025) - backlash, but strong tested scores
- **What:** AI remakes of Coca-Cola's 1995 truck-caravan ad. 2024: made by Secret Level, Silverside AI and Wild Card using Leonardo, Luma and Runway, with Kling added late for human motion (reported). 2025: Secret Level with Silverside AI, using Sora, Veo 3 and Luma; animals instead of people; Silverside says five specialists produced 70,000 raw clips and the schedule dropped from a year to 30 days. A traditionally filmed spot ran alongside.
- **Platform:** TV, YouTube, social, global.
- **Results:** System1 audience testing gave the 2025 ad 5.9 stars (its maximum), "exceptional" spike and 97% distinctiveness; the 2024 ad also scored 5.9. Online sentiment was heavily negative both years: "soulless", "creepy", job-loss criticism; the 2025 YouTube upload drew about 2,300 comments, many negative.
- **Why it went both ways:** *Tie to product and emotion:* the caravan is a decades-old memory asset, so recognition (what System1 measures) stayed high. *Novelty:* "AI made it" became the headline instead of the story. *Emotion:* a brand built on human warmth used a process seen as replacing people at Christmas, the season of giving. *Craft:* 2024 visuals had distorted trucks and odd bottle contents; 2025 improved, but uncanny eyes/skin remained.
- **Lesson:** Testing panels and comment sections measure different things. If the brand's promise is human warmth, AI should be invisible in the result and you should not lead the PR with "AI".

## 2. Toys"R"Us origin film made with Sora (June 2024) - backlash
- **What:** About a one-minute brand film with agency Native Foreign, using pre-release Sora, telling the story of founder Charles Lazarus and Geoffrey the Giraffe; premiered at Cannes Lions. Native Foreign's CCO put Sora's share at roughly 80-85%, with corrective VFX and an original score.
- **Platform:** Cannes screening, then online.
- **Results:** No performance numbers published. Heavy criticism from filmmakers (Joe Russo publicly derided it) and commentators ("abomination", "soulless"); some praise for the experiment.
- **Why it failed:** *Hook:* none beyond "first Sora ad". *Craft:* critics read it as "soulless", and with Sora pre-release plus corrective VFX, holding one human lead across shots was the hardest part of the brief. *Distribution:* launched to the creative industry at Cannes, the most hostile possible audience. *Tie to product:* nostalgic origin story with no product or reason to visit a store.
- **Lesson:** Character consistency is non-negotiable for a story with a human lead; if the tool can't hold a face, change the concept (animals, objects, POV) rather than ship drift.

## 3. Kalshi NBA Finals ad by PJ Accetturo, Veo 3 (June 2025) - success
- **What:** A chaotic, fully AI-generated spot (characters in absurd situations betting on everything) made with Veo 3 by director PJ Accetturo, aired during the YouTube TV stream of NBA Finals Game 3 on 11 June 2025.
- **Platform:** YouTube TV broadcast, then X and social.
- **Results:** NPR reported over 3 million views on Kalshi's X account within a week. Kalshi said prompting costs were under $2,000 (Accetturo's fee undisclosed); a Kalshi employee said a comparable spot would normally cost seven figures. Accetturo reported 300-400 generations for 15 usable clips, edited himself. A "20 million viewers" claim in one outlet is uncorroborated.
- **Why it worked:** *Hook:* every shot is a self-contained absurd joke, so AI weirdness reads as intentional comedy. *Novelty:* "first fully AI ad in the NBA Finals" earned press coverage worth more than the airtime. *Tie to product:* "you can bet on anything" is literally what each scene shows. *Cost:* fast and cheap enough to iterate. *Emotion:* humour, not sentiment, so uncanniness doesn't hurt.
- **Lesson:** Choose a concept where AI's strengths (surreal variety, speed) are the joke and its weaknesses (consistency, warmth) don't matter.

## 4. Under Armour "Forever Is Made Now" with Anthony Joshua (March 2024) - backlash
- **What:** Director Wes Walker billed it the "first AI-powered sports commercial", built from existing assets and a 3D model of Joshua without athlete access.
- **Platform:** Instagram (director's posts).
- **Results:** No performance data. Controversy centred on reused footage from an earlier Under Armour commercial whose director (Gustav Johansson) wasn't initially credited; another cinematographer questioned whether his footage was used. Reaction was divided.
- **Why it failed:** *Novelty claim* invited scrutiny it couldn't survive. *Credit and consent:* the AI layer was seen as a way to repackage other people's work. *Tie to product:* generic athlete montage, not a product idea.
- **Lesson:** Credit every human source and secure rights to every input asset (footage, likeness, 3D model) before claiming "AI-made". Don't lead with a "first ever" claim.

## 5. McDonald's Netherlands AI Christmas ad (December 2025) - pulled
- **What:** A 45-second spot by TBWA\Neboko and The Sweetshop: rapid AI clips of holiday mishaps under the line "the most terrible time of the year".
- **Platform:** YouTube / social, Netherlands.
- **Results:** Live 6 December 2025, removed about three days later after mockery ("creepy", "lazy", "AI slop"). McDonald's NL called it an "important learning"; The Sweetshop's CEO defended it ("This wasn't an AI trick, it was a film").
- **Why it failed:** *Timing:* weeks after Coca-Cola's backlash, audiences were primed to spot AI. *Emotion:* a cynical premise plus uncanny visuals at Christmas. *Craft:* dozens of fast, unrelated clips read as a slop montage. *Tie to product:* the "McDonald's as refuge" link was weak.
- **Lesson:** A fast montage of AI clips is the most recognisable slop format; one strong idea with fewer, better shots survives scrutiny.

## 6. Svedka "Shake Your Bots Off", Super Bowl LX (February 2026) - backlash
- **What:** Billed as the first entirely AI-generated national Super Bowl ad: robots "Fembot" and "Brobot" dancing to "Super Freak"; in-house team with Silverside.
- **Platform:** Super Bowl LX broadcast (8 Feb 2026), social.
- **Results:** No sales or brand-lift data found. Immediate negative social reaction ("AI slop", "Temu I, Robot vibes"). iSpot counted 15 of 66 Super Bowl ads (23%) containing or promoting AI. Svedka later said consumers would make its next Super Bowl ad.
- **Why it failed:** *Novelty:* "first all-AI" was the only idea. *Tie to product:* robots dancing had no link to vodka. *Emotion:* awkward rather than funny. *Distribution:* Super Bowl audiences compare you to the year's best-crafted ads.
- **Lesson:** "Made with AI" is not a concept. If the ad would be weak with real actors, AI won't save it.

## 7. Volvo "Come Back Stronger", Saudi Arabia (March 2025) - mixed
- **What:** Volvo's first fully AI-made ad, for its Saudi return; no car shown. Visuals in Midjourney, production in Runway, human-written script refined with ChatGPT.
- **Platform:** Saudi market, digital.
- **Results:** Agency said timelines dropped from months to weeks (self-reported). Adweek reported controversy. No performance numbers found.
- **Why mixed:** Avoiding the car sidestepped product-fidelity problems (a smart workaround), but a car ad with no car weakens the product tie and invited "is this lazy?" criticism.
- **Lesson:** If AI can't render the product faithfully, show real product (shot or 3D) composited in, rather than removing it.

## 8. Heinz "A.I. Ketchup" (2022, awarded 2023-24) - success
- **What:** Rethink asked DALL-E 2 to draw "ketchup"; it kept drawing something like a Heinz bottle. Heinz turned the outputs into social, OOH and an invitation for people to try their own prompts.
- **Platform:** Social, OOH, DOOH, PR.
- **Results:** Self-reported: initially "850M+ earned impressions", later updated to "over 1.15 billion"; earned media value "2500% more than media investment". 18 OOH boards delivered 55M+ impressions. Cannes Lions entries; WARC Creative 100 listing.
- **Why it worked:** *Tie to product:* the AI output *is* the proof point (even a machine thinks ketchup = Heinz). *Novelty:* early, participatory use of image gen. *Cost:* low production; earned media did the work. *Hook:* one-line insight anyone can repeat.
- **Lesson:** Use AI as evidence for a brand truth, not just as a production shortcut.

## 9. Jacquemus Le Bambino bags on wheels, Paris (April 2023) - success (CGI FOOH)
- **What:** CGI video posted to Instagram on 5 April 2023 showing three giant Bambino bags driving like buses down Rue Auber. Credited (single source) to artist Ian Padgham. Followed a Galeries Lafayette pop-up with oversized props.
- **Platform:** Instagram, then press.
- **Results:** No verified view count found. Widely covered (Hypebeast, Paper, Grazia); many viewers debated whether it was real before Jacquemus confirmed 3D renders.
- **Why it worked:** *Hook:* an impossible but plausible object in a real street, readable in one second. *Tie to product:* the bag itself is the hero, at absurd scale. *Novelty:* "is it real?" drove comments and shares. *Distribution:* fitted a brand already known for surreal stunts.
- **Lesson:** Put the actual product, oversized, into a real, recognisable place; the debate about reality is the share trigger.

## 10. Maybelline Sky High mascara on the London Underground (July 2023) - success (CGI FOOH)
- **What:** Real footage of a London train shot in June with a giant mascara wand added in post, coating huge lashes on the train front. Posted 8 July 2023.
- **Platform:** Instagram, TikTok.
- **Results:** Self-reported (Cannes Lions entry): 150 million views in the first week, 8M+ engagements, +118% Sky High sales uplift in the viral week, from a single CGI video costing £25k. No independent count found.
- **Why it worked:** *Tie to product:* the action is literally what the product does (lengthen lashes). *Hook:* the brush stroke happens in the first seconds. *Cost:* one cheap clip. *Novelty:* many viewers thought the lashes were real.
- **Lesson:** The best FOOH shows the product's benefit as a physical action at giant scale, not just a giant logo.

## 11. Duolingo "Duo is dead" (February 2025) - success (not AI)
- **What:** Duolingo announced its owl mascot's death (staged as hit by a Cybertruck) on 11 February 2025, ran mourning content, then revived Duo on 24 February.
- **Platform:** X, TikTok, Instagram, in-app.
- **Results:** Reported 47M+ views on the announcement by next morning; another source says 50M+ on X. A MrBeast tribute reportedly got 96M views. "1.7 billion impressions" and "record Q1 DAU adds" are single secondary sources - unverified. Brands (Netflix, Baskin Robbins, Cheez-It) joined in.
- **Why it worked:** *Hook:* a shocking, story-shaped premise. *Distribution:* invited other brands and creators to co-create. *Tie to product:* "keep your streak / Duo needs you" pushed lessons. *Emotion:* mock grief, a parasocial mascot people already loved. *Cost:* mostly in-house social.
- **Lesson:** A mascot with years of character equity can carry a multi-week story arc; build the character before the stunt.

## 12. Ryanair TikTok "plane face" (2020-present) - success (not AI)
- **What:** Ryanair's account uses a cheap face filter to put eyes and a mouth on its planes and answers complaints in the same snarky voice.
- **Platform:** TikTok.
- **Results:** No verified follower count found. Started May 2020 with a small in-house team and near-zero media spend (single blog source). A 2025 academic study of Ryanair's Twitter found its humour reframed customer irritation as part of the brand experience.
- **Why it worked:** *Hook:* a talking plane is instantly recognisable. *Tie to product:* jokes about cheapness and strict bag rules, the brand's real truths. *Emotion:* self-deprecation disarms complaints (and it reportedly avoids mocking people with cancelled flights). *Cost:* lo-fi on purpose.
- **Lesson:** Lean into the thing customers already joke about; lo-fi and native beats polished.

## 13. Scrub Daddy on TikTok (2021-present) - success (not AI)
- **What:** Founder-led, mascot-heavy, absurd product content plus creator collaborations (including with Duolingo).
- **Platform:** TikTok.
- **Results:** 2022: CEO said sales growing about 30% a year with "demonstrable" growth since joining TikTok; 1.4M followers then. Later claims (4.2M followers, $220M revenue) come from an agency blog - unverified. The company admitted retail attribution was hard to measure.
- **Why it worked:** *Tie to product:* demos of the sponge changing texture are inherently watchable. *Distribution:* treated the account as a media property; collabs borrowed audiences. *Emotion:* weird, playful character.
- **Lesson:** A visible product transformation is a built-in hook; film it repeatedly in new contexts.

## 14. Liquid Death "Murder Your Thirst" (2019 origin, still running) - success (pre-range, included for its lesson)
- **What:** Founder Mike Cessario made a low-budget fake commercial for canned water before the product existed, run as a Facebook ad.
- **Platform:** Facebook, YouTube.
- **Results:** Reported figures conflict: "3 million views" before launch vs BevNET's "over 1 million views in less than two months". An ad-intelligence page (2026) says the ad has run on Facebook since November 2020.
- **Why it worked:** *Novelty:* heavy-metal branding on the most boring product category. *Hook:* the absurd contrast. *Cost:* tiny. *Distribution:* used the ad to validate demand before production.
- **Lesson:** A cheap video can test positioning before you build. AI video now makes this "ad before product" test even cheaper.

## Patterns across cases

1. **"Made with AI" is never the idea.** Every AI failure here (Toys"R"Us, Under Armour, McDonald's NL, Svedka) led with "first AI..." or let AI become the headline. The AI successes (Kalshi, Heinz) had an idea that would work in any medium, and AI either made it affordable or *was* the proof.
2. **Match the concept to AI's strengths.** Surreal variety, comedy and impossible scale work (Kalshi, Jacquemus, Maybelline). Warmth, human faces and continuity of a lead character fail (Coca-Cola, Toys"R"Us).
3. **Emotion and season matter.** Christmas and nostalgia amplify "soulless" criticism; brands built on human warmth pay the highest penalty.
4. **Tie the visual to the product benefit.** Maybelline's brush coats lashes; Heinz's AI draws Heinz; Kalshi shows betting on anything. Weak ties (Svedka's robots, Volvo's no-car ad, Under Armour's generic montage) underperform or draw criticism.
5. **"Is it real?" is a share engine** (FOOH), but only when the illusion is clean; AI artefacts flip the debate to "is it lazy?".
6. **Native, lo-fi and character-led beats polish on social** (Ryanair, Duolingo, Scrub Daddy). Character equity compounds over years.
7. **Cost is real, but so is waste.** Kalshi: under $2,000 in prompts but 300-400 generations for 15 clips; Coca-Cola 2025: 70,000 clips. Budget for selection and editing time, not just generation.
8. **Credit, rights and consent are reputational risk.** Under Armour shows that unclear asset provenance becomes the story.
9. **Panels and comment sections disagree.** Coca-Cola scored top marks in System1 testing while trending for backlash. Decide in advance which you're optimising for, and prepare a response.
10. **Distribution context sets the bar.** Cannes and the Super Bowl invite expert comparison; a feed rewards speed and novelty.
