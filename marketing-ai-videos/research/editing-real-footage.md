# Editing real event / booth footage into scroll-stoppers (2026-11-05)

Quick research pass. Sources are mostly vendors and agencies, so treat these as craft conventions, not tested data.

## Techniques
- **Speed ramps over cuts.** Ramp one continuous shot (real time → 300% → real time) on a beat. It surprises more than a cut, and it hides shaky handheld walk-ins. Time every ramp to movement and the music, or it reads as a CapCut template. [Morphic glossary][1], [freelancer brief][2]
- **Kinetic type is the lead visual on muted feeds** (LinkedIn, IG). Type *interacts* with the footage (tracked to walls, occluded by people walking past, wiping with camera moves), not floating on top. [Graphic Design Junction 2026][3], [Tangence 2026][4]
- **Mixed media beats pure gloss in 2026:** grain, imperfect frames and jump cuts alongside polished hero shots. [Graphic Design Junction][3]
- **Shoot or deliver horizontal 4K; cut 9:16 from it.** Vertical masters look soft on LED walls; horizontal 4K reframes cleanly to 9:16 / 4:5. [IRPR][5]
- **Booth-loop logic for social:** structure as 12-15 s micro-segments that each stand alone, since people land mid-stream. [IRPR][5]
- **Match cuts** (not sourced; craft standard): line up shapes, light sources or camera direction between two different clients' stands so one stand "becomes" another. This is the device that makes a portfolio reel feel designed rather than compiled.

## Rules for Markable footage
1. Never open on a wide, static, empty booth. Open on motion, a detail or a person stopping.
2. One stand per beat, cut to the music grid; speed-ramp into each reveal.
3. Track type into the space (onto a wall, a fascia, the floor) at least once. That's the "who made this?" moment.
4. Grade all clients' footage to one look so 20 different stands read as one studio.
5. Client logos on stands are fine (that's the proof). Never AI-alter them.
