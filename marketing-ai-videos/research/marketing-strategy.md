# Marketing strategy playbook: niche, unique, and still at scale

Researched 2026-11-05. Scope: strategy only. Platform specs, ad mechanics, psychology, AI tools and case studies live in their own files in `research/`. Numbers marked "(vendor data, unverified)" come from a company selling something related; treat them as directional. Source numbers in [brackets] refer to the list at the end.

**The one-paragraph version.** Be narrow in *who you are for and how you look*; be broad in *who sees you*. Win positioning with a sharp "only we..." claim against the real alternative, make every video carry distinctive brand assets so reach compounds into memory, let the algorithm find the audience from creative diversity rather than targeting, and measure demand with "how did you hear about us?" as well as clicks. Originality is a process (constraints, outside references, kill lists), not a mood.

---

## 1. Positioning and differentiation

### The frameworks, compressed

- **Obviously Awesome (April Dunford).** Positioning has five components: competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target customers, market category [8]. Start with the alternative, not the competitor list: "what would our best clients do if we didn't exist?" For an AI-video agency the honest answer is rarely "another AI-video agency"; it is "a traditional production house", "the in-house designer with CapCut", or "do nothing and post static images". Unique attributes only count relative to that alternative.
- **Category design (Play Bigger).** Their claim: category kings captured about 76% of the market cap in the categories they studied (35 tech category kings) [9]. A book-era sample, not a law, but the lesson holds: if you name and define the problem, you get remembered as the answer. Small-scale version: coin a name for the specific thing you make (a format, a method) and use it everywhere.
- **Blue Ocean (Kim and Mauborgne).** Use the ERRC grid: what does the category over-serve that we Eliminate or Reduce, and what do we Raise or Create that nobody offers [10]? Example for AI video: eliminate shoot days and location fees, reduce revision rounds via pre-approved style frames, raise volume of cuts per idea, create a "weekly test pack" product. An empty Eliminate box means you are just adding cost.
- **Only-ness (Marty Neumeier, *Zag*).** One sentence that captures what only you do; if you can't write it, you don't have a brand yet [11]. Working template: *"We are the only [category] that [benefit] for [specific audience] in [specific place/moment] during [trend]."* Test: if a competitor could sign the sentence unchanged, rewrite it. This is the same test as `TASTE.md` rule 2 (logo-swap test).
- **How Brands Grow (Byron Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass).** Brands grow mostly by adding buyers, many of them light or occasional buyers, which makes broad salience matter [5]. Two jobs: **mental availability** (being thought of in buying situations, built through links to *category entry points*, the cues and occasions that trigger the category) and **physical availability** (being easy to find and buy). Distinctive brand assets (colours, sounds, characters, type, a voice) are the shortcuts that make exposure stick to *your* name.
- **Long and Short (Binet and Field, IPA).** Rule of thumb from the IPA databank: roughly 60% brand building, 40% activation for consumer brands [7]. Their B2B cut is closer to 46/54 because B2B is "a bit more rational, a bit more activation-heavy" [6]. Corollary from LinkedIn's B2B Institute and John Dawes: only around 5% of B2B buyers are in-market in a given quarter (the "95:5 rule"), so most of your audience can't buy now but will shortlist the one to three brands they already know when they can [4].

### The tension, stated honestly

"Be super niche" (our TASTE.md) and "reach light buyers broadly" (Sharp) look like a contradiction. They are not, once you separate three layers:

| Layer | Be niche | Be broad |
|---|---|---|
| **Positioning** (who it's for, what only we do) | Yes. One audience, one problem, one claim. | No |
| **Creative** (the idea, the look, the hook) | Yes. Specific situations and insider detail are what make strangers stop; specificity reads as competence. | No |
| **Distribution** (who sees it) | No. Don't over-target. | Yes. Broad audiences, many placements, let the algorithm and shares find the people who self-select. |

Practical resolution:
1. **Niche in content, broad in delivery.** A video about one very specific pain ("the Friday 5pm brief from a franchise marketing manager") will still be served broadly; the niche detail is what earns attention and self-selects the right viewer. On Meta this is now literally how delivery works: Meta's own 2025 language moved from "niche targeting" to "creative diversification" as the main lever for reaching relevant audiences [1][2].
2. **Niche should mean one category entry point, not one tiny audience.** Pick CEPs ("launching a product next month", "our ads are fatigued", "we need 30 cuts by Monday") and own them one at a time.
3. **Distinctive assets are non-negotiable even when every video looks different.** TASTE.md demands a different visual idea per video; Sharp demands consistency. Resolve it by keeping a small fixed kit constant (a sonic logo, a type treatment, a colour, a recurring character or framing device, the voice) and varying everything else. Different idea, same fingerprints.
4. **Budget split.** For the agency's own marketing (B2B): roughly half to brand-building content that will never ask for anything, half to activation (offers, retargeting, case studies, outbound). For a DTC client, lean 60/40 brand [6][7]. Revisit by category; these are starting points, not rules.

## 2. Offers and conversion

- **Hormozi's value equation** (from *$100M Offers*): perceived value = (dream outcome x perceived likelihood) / (time delay x effort and sacrifice) [12]. It is a thinking tool, not math. For an AI-video service the cheap levers are time delay (first cut in 72 hours) and effort (no shoot, no brief longer than one page, we write the scripts). Likelihood is raised by proof: case-study cuts, named results, guarantees.
- **Grand Slam Offer.** Stack the core service with things that kill each objection (e.g. "30 cuts per concept", "hook test report", "captions in 3 languages", "free re-cut if hook rate misses target"), give it a name, and make price comparison hard because no one else sells exactly that bundle.
- **Risk reversal.** Pick one you can afford: paid pilot credited against a retainer, "re-cut free until hold rate beats your current best ad", or a no-lock-in month one. Specific, measurable guarantees beat "satisfaction guaranteed".
- **Single CTA.** Each video and each landing page asks for one action. Two asks split intent; zero asks wastes the moment. Brand-building videos may have a soft CTA ("follow for the next one").
- **Message match.** The landing page headline repeats the ad's promise in the same words and visual, ideally a still from the same video. A mismatch reads as bait and kills conversion before the copy is read.
- **Lenses for scripting** (pick one per video, don't mix):
  - **AIDA** (attention, interest, desire, action) for product reveals and launches.
  - **PAS** (problem, agitate, solution) for pain-led performance ads. Agitate with specifics, not adjectives.
  - **JTBD** (the job the buyer is hiring you for, including the emotional job: "make me look good to my boss") for positioning and B2B. Most agency buyers are hiring "never be embarrassed by a missed deadline" as much as "great video".
- **Social proof.** Ranked by strength: named client + number + face > named client logo > anonymous testimonial > follower counts. Video testimonials should be shot (or built) to the same craft standard as the work; a weak testimonial undermines a strong portfolio.

## 3. Demand generation, not just lead generation

- **Create vs capture.** Lead gen captures the 5% already in-market; demand gen makes the other 95% know you exist before they need you [4]. Gated PDFs and "book a call" ads only work on the 5%.
- **Dark social is real and under-measured.** Refine Labs' 12-month study (620 conversions, $21.5M closed-won ARR) found a roughly 90% gap between software attribution and what customers said; podcasts were credited with 53% of revenue by self-report and 0% by software [13]. Vendor data on its own clients, but the direction matches every agency owner's experience: people see you in feeds, DMs, Slack groups and podcasts, then arrive "direct" or via branded search.
- **Self-reported attribution.** Add a required open-text "How did you hear about us?" field to every contact form and discovery call script [13]. Read it weekly. Run it alongside software attribution (Refine Labs calls the combination "hybrid attribution"). Don't use a dropdown; the free-text answers are where you learn which video or person is working.
- **Creator-led / founder-led content.** Buyers follow people more than logos. The founder (or a named creative director) publishes the agency's thinking, behind-the-scenes, teardowns and opinions 3-5x a week on LinkedIn. AI video helps here: the founder can appear as presenter in a consistent, high-craft format without daily shoots (disclose AI where platforms or clients require it).
- **Employee advocacy on LinkedIn.** Employee posts tend to outperform company-page posts; one third-party analysis of 700M LinkedIn impressions claims about 14x (vendor data, unverified) [27]. Widely repeated "8x" and "561%" figures trace to Edelman and Sprout Social rather than LinkedIn itself; don't quote them as LinkedIn data. Practical: give staff ready-made cuts and a reason to post (their own work, their own voice), never identical copy-pasted captions.
- **Thought Leader Ads** on LinkedIn let you boost an individual's organic post as an ad. Third-party blogs claim they out-click brand ads; I found no first-party number, so test it yourself. Use them to put money behind posts that already earned organic engagement.

## 4. Content distribution systems

- **One idea, many cuts.** Brief one strong idea, then deliberately produce: a hero (30-90 s), 5-10 shorts with different hooks, 3 aspect ratios, a still/carousel set, a behind-the-scenes "how we made it", and a teardown post. The idea is the expensive part; cuts are cheap with AI. Different hooks on the same body are the fastest learning loop you have.
- **Cadence evidence.** Buffer's analysis of 2.1M Instagram posts from 102K accounts found the biggest gain moving from 1-2 to 3-5 posts a week, with smaller gains above that; missed weeks dragged growth below the account's own baseline [14]. Their TikTok analysis of 11.4M posts found 2-5 posts a week gave up to 17% more views per post [14]; they publish a similar LinkedIn study [15]. Correlational, Buffer-user data, but consistent: **consistency beats bursts, and 3-5 a week is the practical floor per main channel.**
- **Creative testing velocity.** On Meta, Andromeda (announced Dec 2024) reversed the old logic: creative now decides who sees the ad [2]. What counts as "diverse" is concept-level difference (different story, format, face, setting), not swapped backgrounds or text overlays [1][3]. Figures like "10+ variations per campaign" or "70% visual difference" circulate but I could not find Meta confirming them; treat as folklore. Practical rule: ship a new *concept* (not variant) every week per active account, and kill or scale on hook rate and cost per result after enough spend to read.
- **Hook library.** Keep a living spreadsheet: hook text, first-frame description, device (pattern break, question, claim, reveal, wrong-then-right, POV, "you're doing X wrong"), platform, hook rate, hold rate, date. Tag each by category entry point. Before every brief, pull the three best hooks for that CEP and *write one that beats them*. Retire hooks after fatigue, and never reuse the same visual opening twice in a row (TASTE.md rule 4).
- **"Creative is the new targeting."** It's a slogan, not a Meta term [1][2], but it is the right operating model: broad audience, Advantage+ style delivery, and diversity of concepts as your "audience segments". Each concept speaks to one CEP or persona; the algorithm finds the people.

## 5. Search in 2026: SEO plus answer engines

- **Clicks are leaking to AI answers.** Pew's browsing study (900 US adults, March 2025) found people clicked a traditional result 8% of the time when an AI Overview appeared vs 15% without; links inside the summary got about 1%; and sessions ended on the results page more often [16]. Google disputes the methodology. Either way: the answer itself is now the shelf, so **being cited is the new ranking.**
- **What Google says.** Google's own guidance: AI Overviews and AI Mode run on core Search ranking and quality systems; there is no special AI markup; make unique, non-commodity, people-first content, keep technical SEO clean, and use preview controls (nosnippet, max-snippet) if you want to limit use [18].
- **What the research says (GEO).** The Princeton/Georgia Tech/AI2/IIT Delhi GEO paper (KDD 2024) found adding citations, quotations and statistics boosted visibility in generative answers by up to about 40% on their benchmark [17]. A lab benchmark and an upper bound, not a promise, but it matches common sense: engines quote pages that contain quotable, specific, sourced facts.
- **Playbook for an agency:**
  1. Publish pages that answer the questions buyers type into ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Mode: "how much does an AI video ad cost", "AI video agency for [niche]", "[tool] vs [tool] for product videos". Lead with a direct answer in the first two sentences, then specifics.
  2. Include your own data (e.g. hook-rate results across 50 client tests), named examples and prices; original numbers are what gets cited.
  3. Embed each video with a transcript, descriptive title and chapter markers so the video itself is indexable.
  4. Get mentioned elsewhere: answer engines lean on third-party sources (reviews, roundups, Reddit, YouTube, podcasts). Earned mentions are now an SEO activity.
  5. Test monthly: ask the main engines your 20 money questions and log who gets cited.
- **YouTube and TikTok are search engines.** Google's own SVP said in 2022 that almost 40% of 18-24-year-olds looking for lunch go to TikTok or Instagram instead of Maps or Search (internal Google figure, a specific use case) [19]. Title, on-screen text, spoken words and captions should contain the phrase a viewer would search. Make some "searchable" evergreen videos (how-to, comparisons, costs) alongside the scroll-stopping ones.

## 6. Community, word of mouth and referral loops (brief)

- **STEPPS** (Jonah Berger, *Contagious*): Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories [20]. Design each video for at least two. For B2B the strongest pair is usually social currency ("sharing this makes me look sharp") plus practical value ("my team needs this").
- **Build a share moment in.** TASTE.md asks for one screenshot-able beat; make it a frame that contains the brand fingerprint.
- **Referral loop for the agency.** After each delivered project: (1) a co-branded case-study cut the client is proud to post, (2) a named credit on the client's post, (3) a referral offer (e.g. one free concept for a referral that signs). The client's post reaches exactly the peers you want.
- **Community.** A small, useful space (a monthly "hook teardown" live, a private group for in-house marketers in your niche) compounds faster than a big generic one. Members become the dark-social channel you can't buy.

## 7. Creative strategy for originality

### What the 2025-2026 Grand Prix work has in common

- **2026 Film Grand Prix:** Anthropic/Claude, "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude" by Mother London; Super Bowl spots that mocked advertising inside AI chatbots [21]. Jury president Pelle Sjoenell said it "reshuffled the perceptions in the most consequential technology race of our age" [21]. Pattern: **anti-ad**, a sharp category-level "only we" claim delivered as comedy. (Counterpoint worth reading: creatives warned the anti-ad stance could age poorly [29].)
- **2026 Titanium Grand Prix:** Suncorp "Haven" by Leo Australia, a climate-resilience platform: an insurer doing something useful rather than saying something [22].
- **2026 Titanium Lion:** Oreo Cows by VML: built a brand icon out of the Belted Galloway cow, a real animal that already looks like an Oreo [23]. Pattern: **find the brand in the world** (a coincidence nobody owns yet) instead of inventing a metaphor.
- **2025:** Titanium GP to AXA "Three Words" (Publicis Conseil); Film GP shared by L'Oréal Paris "The Final Copy of Ilon Specht" and Channel 4 "Considering What?" for the Paralympics [24]. Pattern: brand history and product truth turned into a cultural story; confrontational, specific, not pretty.
- **FOOH (fake out-of-home):** Jacquemus' CGI Bambino bags driving through Paris (April 2023) made CGI that looks like a real stunt into a format [25]. By 2026 it is a crowded genre; use it only when the impossible object *is* the product truth, and add a twist the viewer hasn't seen.

Recurring patterns: a real product or category truth; one simple device explainable in a sentence; specificity over polish; an enemy or tension; earned media as the real distribution.

### A repeatable process for non-obvious ideas

1. **Write the truth first.** One sentence of product truth + one of audience tension. No idea work until both are written.
2. **Map the category's conventions** (collect 20 competitor ads, list what they all do: colours, openers, music, claims). That list becomes your **kill list**: you may not use any of it.
3. **Constraints.** Force 3 constrained routes per brief, e.g. "no people", "one continuous shot", "only the product's own materials", "silent with captions", "10 seconds, one joke", "shot as if it were a nature documentary".
4. **Steal from outside the category.** For each brief, pull references from two unrelated worlds (museum signage, sports broadcast graphics, cooking shows, airline safety videos, scientific diagrams). The transplant is where originality comes from.
5. **Find the brand in the world** (Oreo Cows logic): what already looks, sounds or behaves like the product?
6. **Lo-fi vs hi-fi as a deliberate choice.** Lo-fi (phone-native, raw, creator-style) wins trust and feels native in feeds; hi-fi wins "who made this?". The strongest pieces often put a hi-fi impossible moment inside a lo-fi frame (the FOOH trick). Decide per objective, not per habit.
7. **Score before you make.** Rate each route 1-5 on: logo-swap test, explainable in one sentence, screenshot moment, tied to product truth, distinctive asset visible. Make the top one, plus the wildest one.

### Avoiding sameness and AI slop

- AI defaults converge: same lighting, same glossy faces, same slow push-ins, same "epic" music. Treat every model default as a convention on the kill list.
- Lock a **style bible per client** (palette, lens language, texture, motion rules, type) before generating; generate from the bible, not from the prompt alone.
- Use AI for the impossible, not the ordinary. If it could be shot on a phone in ten minutes, an AI version looks like slop.
- Human taste checkpoints at concept, style frame and final cut. Check for artefacts (TASTE.md rule 7) frame by frame on the hero shots.
- Specific beats generic: real product, real places, real numbers, real names (with permission).

### Briefing method (fits `templates/video-brief.md`)

One page. Objective (one metric) - audience (one person, one moment, one CEP) - the single-minded proposition (one sentence, passes the only-ness test) - truth and tension - kill list - mandatory distinctive assets - constraints - the share moment - CTA - what "wow" looks like here (a reference from outside the category). If the proposition needs "and", it's two briefs.

## 8. Agency-specific: winning clients with video

- **Proof-of-work beats claims.** Post the work, the process and the results in public. Every client project should yield: the final, a 15-30 s "before/after" (brief vs result), a breakdown of how it was made, and the numbers once available.
- **Spec work with a purpose.** Make unsolicited concepts for brands in your niche (clearly labelled as spec, not affiliated, never using their branding in a way that implies endorsement or that they commissioned it) and post the thinking behind them. It's a portfolio piece and an outbound opener.
- **Case-study video structure:** the client's problem in their words - the constraint - the idea in one line - the work - one number - one quote. 60-90 s, captioned, plus 3 short cuts.
- **Niche specialisation.** "AI video for agencies" is still too broad. Pick a vertical (e.g. DTC skincare, B2B SaaS launches, real estate developers) or a format (product launches, ad-testing packs). Specialists can charge more and get referred more because they're easy to describe. Own one niche fully before adding a second.
- **B2B LinkedIn specifics.**
  - Founder and team profiles carry reach; the company page is the archive.
  - Native video with burned-in captions; the first line of the post and the first frame must work together.
  - Mix per week: 1 work showcase, 1 teardown/opinion, 1 process/behind-the-scenes, 1 result/case study, 1 personal or culture post.
  - LinkedIn video is growing as a B2B channel; a Wistia report ranks LinkedIn above YouTube for B2B video (vendor data, unverified) [28]. Third-party benchmarks commonly show video ads with lower CTR than single-image ads, so judge LinkedIn video on reach, memory and self-reported attribution, not CTR.
  - Boost proven organic posts via Thought Leader Ads; retarget video viewers with a case study, then the offer.
  - Comment on target clients' posts with substance before ever pitching them.

## 9. Measurement: what to track at each stage

Definitions differ between tools; always state the formula next to the number.

| Stage | Metric | Formula / note | Benchmark (sourced only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop | **Hook rate** (thumbstop) | 3-second views / impressions | Motion: 20-40% "generally solid", varies wildly by industry [26] (vendor data, unverified) |
| Hold | **Hold rate** | ThruPlays (or 15 s views) / 3 s views; formulas vary | Motion: strong Meta creative 25%+ [26] (vendor data, unverified) |
| Watch | **Average watch %** / retention curve | Find the drop-off second and fix that beat | None reliable; compare against your own account |
| Spread | **Share rate**, save rate | Shares / views; saves / views | Best leading signal of organic reach and word of mouth; compare internally |
| Click | **CTR** (link) | Link clicks / impressions | No reliable cross-industry figure found; compare to your own account by format |
| Convert | **CVR** | Conversions / landing sessions | Message match is the first fix |
| Economics | **CAC**, **LTV**, LTV:CAC, payback | Blended CAC (all spend / new clients) beats channel CAC | Set by your margins, not industry averages |
| Demand | Self-reported source, branded search volume, direct traffic, inbound share | Weekly read of "how did you hear about us?" [13] | Trend over time |
| Memory | Brand recall / mention in AI answers | Monthly prompt test across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode | Trend over time |

Rules:
- Diagnose in funnel order: bad hook rate = opening problem; good hook, bad hold = middle problem; good hold, bad CTR = offer/CTA problem; good CTR, bad CVR = landing page/message-match problem.
- Don't kill brand content on CTR. Judge it on reach, watch time, shares, branded search and what people say in discovery calls.
- Report to clients with one north-star metric per campaign and at most three supporting metrics.

## 10. Strategy checklist before any video (one page)

**Positioning**
- [ ] Who exactly is this for (one person, one moment, one category entry point)?
- [ ] What's the real alternative they'd use instead?
- [ ] Single-minded proposition in one sentence. Does it pass the "only we..." / logo-swap test?

**Objective**
- [ ] Brand-building or activation? (Not both.) One north-star metric chosen.
- [ ] Where it sits in the 60/40 (or B2B ~50/50) mix.

**Idea**
- [ ] Product/category truth and audience tension written down.
- [ ] Category conventions listed and banned (kill list), including AI defaults.
- [ ] At least one constraint and one outside-the-category reference used.
- [ ] Explainable in one sentence; contains a screenshot/share moment.
- [ ] Visuals are the subject, not decoration (TASTE.md rule 5).

**Brand memory**
- [ ] Distinctive assets present (sonic, colour, type, character, voice) even though the idea is new.
- [ ] Brand identifiable with the sound off and with the sound on.

**Distribution**
- [ ] Broad delivery planned; niche lives in the creative.
- [ ] Cut plan: hero + 5-10 hook variants + 3 ratios + stills + behind-the-scenes.
- [ ] Searchable title, on-screen words, captions and transcript containing the phrase people search.
- [ ] Who posts it: founder, team, client co-post, boosted as Thought Leader Ad?

**Conversion**
- [ ] One CTA. Landing page headline and visual match the video.
- [ ] Offer passes the value equation; risk reversal stated.
- [ ] Proof included (named result, face, number).

**Measurement**
- [ ] Hook, hold, watch %, share rate, CTR, CVR targets set against the account's own baseline.
- [ ] "How did you hear about us?" field live.
- [ ] Results logged in the hook library after 7 days.
