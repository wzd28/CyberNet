# TASTE.md — standing preferences (read before every project)

> Rule for Claude: read this file in full before starting or continuing any project.
> After every round of feedback on a video, append a dated entry under **Feedback log**
> and, if the feedback is a pattern rather than a one-off, promote it into **Likes** / **Dislikes**.

## Non-negotiables
Every video must be:
1. **Super niche.** Built for one specific viewer with one specific problem, not "businesses" or "everyone".
2. **Unique.** Unlike what others in the category are doing. If a competitor could run it with their logo swapped in, it fails.
3. **Eye-catching with a wow factor.** Frame one has to earn the next second, and there must be at least one moment people would screenshot or send to a friend.
4. **Distinct.** Each video gets its own visual idea, device or format. No recycled template look across videos.
5. **Tied to its topic.** The visuals *are* the subject (the product, the service, the craft), never decoration laid over it.
6. **Built for maximum reach.** Native format per platform, a hook in the first 1–3 s, a loop or rewatch reason, and a share or save trigger.
7. **Thorough and clean.** No AI artefacts (melting hands, warped text, morphing logos), no typos, safe-zone-correct captions, clean audio, and the brand used correctly.

## Production standard (set 2026-11-05)
- **Every output is 4K** (3840x2160 for 16:9, 2160x3840 for 9:16), with high-bitrate masters plus platform-optimised exports.
- **AI voiceover** on every video (ElevenLabs-class quality: never robotic, never default "narrator" cadence).
- **Motion graphics** are a core ingredient, not an afterthought: kinetic type, brand-colour systems, purposeful transitions.
- **"Wow", no exceptions.** The bar: someone watches it and asks "who made this?" and wants to watch it again.
- **Uniqueness above everything.** When given instructions, don't execute them literally: research, then find the unexpected angle and come back with something nobody has seen.

## Clients
Every video is filed under its agency: `projects/markable/` or `projects/cybernet-ai/`. New agencies get their own folder.

## Hard bans (until told otherwise)
- Stock-footage look; generic "AI business" montages (glowing brains, handshakes, holographic dashboards, people pointing at screens).
- Talking heads, unless the talking head *is* the wow factor.
- Opening on a logo, "Hey guys", or "In today's video…".
- Generic AI voiceover cadence, or royalty-free "corporate uplifting" music.

## Likes
- **Real client material over anything synthetic.** As soon as the client's own renders and footage arrived, they replaced the CG placeholders. Always ask for and use the real assets first.

## Likes (B2B / agency clients)
- **Formal, organized, premium.** For B2B clients like exhibition contractors, "wow" means editorial polish, not gimmicks: clean typography hierarchy, a calm rhythm, the client's work as the hero.
- **Show off the client's craft and process** (design → build → delivery) and their big-name clients (credits under each piece).

## Dislikes
- **Gimmicks and games** ("remember one" quiz, viewer tests) and **playful/childish devices** (hearts bursting, cartoon UI) for B2B. Creative ≠ cute.
- **Altering the client's logo.** Use it *exactly as supplied*: same colours, same artwork, on a background it was designed for (Markable: blue + green on white). No white/inverted versions, no recolouring, no AI regeneration. If the supplied file is low-res, rebuild it at high res in the exact sampled colours and verify side by side.
- **Inventing copy on top of the client's.** When the agency supplies headlines, sub-lines and buttons, they appear **verbatim**: same wording, casing, punctuation and button label. Creative hooks may sit around them, but the brief's text is never replaced or paraphrased.
- **Waiting on questions when I could act.** Do the work; ask only when truly blocked, with one clear fix.
